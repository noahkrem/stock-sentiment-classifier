High
Difficulty/grading risk from dropping NLP
Your original proposal lists NLP as a core technique. A pure numerical classifier is simpler to build and may read as lower-difficulty to a grader. Mitigation: confirm with the rubric/TA, or explicitly frame the aggregate-index construction and multi-source feature engineering as your difficulty driver in the report.
High
Index selection may not actually predict AMZN moves
Broad volatility indexes (VIX, sector ETFs) are lagging/coincident indicators, not leading ones — there's a real chance the aggregate index has weak correlation with next-week AMZN return. Mitigation: Jag should sanity-check correlation between each index and AMZN return before Sprint 1 training starts, not after. A quick pandas .corr() check during index selection (Sprint 0) can catch this early.
Medium
10-year data availability per index
Some volatility/sector indexes may not have 10 years of history, or have gaps. Mitigation: Oliver should verify each ticker's earliest available date in yfinance before committing to the full 5-index list — swap out any with under ~5 years of history.
Medium
"Aggregate index" construction is arbitrary unless justified
Simply averaging 5 normalized indexes is a modeling choice that needs justification (equal weighting vs. correlation-based weighting vs. PCA). Mitigation: keep it simple (normalized average) for Milestone 1, but document the choice and mention weighting alternatives as future work — this also doubles as evidence of technical depth for the difficulty criterion.
Low
Scaler / model version mismatch
Same risk as before, different artifact: predict.py must load the exact StandardScaler fitted during training, not refit one — otherwise feature scale will mismatch what the model learned.
